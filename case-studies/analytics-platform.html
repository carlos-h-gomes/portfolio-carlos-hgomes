Case study 01 · Data platform

Replacing a third-party analytics platform

I built the backend and migration path for an internal analytics product that reduced vendor dependency, moved an existing enterprise customer base, and made future onboarding easier.

The challenge

Analytics depended on an external product that limited control.

The existing platform served 18 enterprise customers. Product changes depended on a third party, and the cost grew with the customer base. The replacement had to preserve reporting continuity while giving the internal team control over data collection, configuration, and future product evolution.

What I owned

The backend and the path from customer data to dashboards.

  • Designed and developed the backend services and configuration flows.
  • Connected the product to existing customer and timeline data through APIs.
  • Implemented collection and database-population routines for analytics workloads.
  • Containerized the deployment pattern so the solution could be replicated across environments.
  • Supported the migration of the existing customer base and the onboarding of new customers.

Architecture

A clear boundary between source data, ingestion, storage, and presentation.

Source systemsCustomer and operational data exposed through APIs
Python servicesCollection, normalization, configuration, and orchestration
PostgreSQLStructured analytics storage and customer-level datasets
SupersetDashboards and reusable analytics views

Key decisions

Build for internal ownership, not just feature parity.

  • API-first data accessKept data collection separate from dashboard presentation and made new sources easier to integrate.
  • PostgreSQL as the stable coreProvided a relational layer the team could inspect, maintain, and extend.
  • Superset for presentationAvoided rebuilding mature dashboard capabilities while retaining control of the data product.
  • Containerized replicationMade environment setup more repeatable across multiple platform instances.

Outcome

The company moved from vendor dependence to a platform it could evolve.

The internal product replaced the previous analytics service for 18 customers and supported six additional onboardings. Beyond the direct financial effect, the team gained control over feature evolution, deployment, data pipelines, and the technical foundation for future analytics and AI work.

Confidentiality: Company, vendor, customer, and contract details are intentionally omitted. The published figures use conservative values from internal operational evidence.
